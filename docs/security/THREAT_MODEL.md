# Threat model

Version 21, 9 November 2026 (revised for ARCHITECTURE revisions 11 to 26: D35 to D38 and D40 to D51; T59 to T70 added after gate 3 part C and the design audits), for the design in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`. Update it with every design change, and have `security-auditor` review each update.

## Assets

| Asset | Where it lives | Worst outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Joiner and creator funds | Strategy contract accounts, Rhea positions | Stolen, locked, or paid out wrongly |
| Agent function-call keys | Agent host secrets | Used to take actions outside the rules |
| Full-access keys (agent and strategy accounts) | Held offline by the lead | Account takeover or code replacement |
| Strategy rules before close | Encrypted on-chain; key `K` held by the creator and deliverer | Leaked early, so the creator's edge is lost |
| Viewing-key payments | Escrow in the strategy contract | Paid without delivery, or refunded twice |
| Decision records and attestations | Agent host, published at close | Altered, so the check page is misled |
| Trust in the check page | Static page plus public RPC | Says "matching" when the agent broke the rules |

## Actors

- **Outsider:** any NEAR account. Can call any public method and send any token.
- **Malicious joiner or follower:** has shares or a key purchase, and tries to take more than their due.
- **Compromised agent host:** holds the agent's function-call keys.
- **Compromised deliverer host:** a separate host, holding the deliverer's function-call key and the strategy key `K`.
- **Manipulated model:** private AI output that is wrong, adversarial or prompt-injected.
- **Malicious or buggy token or Rhea:** external contracts behaving unexpectedly, failing, or refunding partially.
- **Pool price manipulator:** moves the pool price within a block to distort valuation.
- **Insider:** a team member with a full-access key.
- **Malicious data source:** an RPC endpoint or indexer feeding the check page false data.

| T82 | **Event index reused after a dropped exit record** (final audit, contract M1; accepted under D52, no contract change after the freeze): `drop_unproductive_exit` pops a failed or refused exit's record, but its `action` and `action_result` events for index N were already emitted; the next action takes `index = actions.len()` = N and emits a second `action` event with the same index and another `op_id`. | T83 | **A failed remove that opens the settle window leaves no record** (final audit, contract M2; accepted under D52): a `Failed("remove")` result opens the settle window because tokens may have moved, yet the record is dropped as unproductive; the only trace is two events. | T84 | **The same pairing is implemented in four places** (final audit, rules M1; accepted): `isPairedOpen`, the contract's `is_paired_open`, the agent's observation and the page's `run.ts` each apply the pair window; they agree on the shipped tests, with no test that they agree on every input. | T86 | **A bounced credit to a deleted account stays in `near_owed` until withdrawn** (viewing keys, D57; accepted, D52): if the creator's or a buyer's account no longer exists, the payout or refund fails, the NEAR is held in `near_owed[account]` and in `near_owed_total`, and the strategy's storage reserve treats it as not spendable. ## Out of scope for the hackathon

Full anonymity, hiding pool positions, preventing key resale, and MEV-style manipulation beyond the caps above. All of these are stated as limits in the README.
